About the Date Calculator

The Date Calculator adds or subtracts a combination of years, months and days from a starting date, then tells you the resulting date, its weekday and how far away it is from today.

Years and months are applied as calendar steps first, then days, so nothing is converted into a fixed block of 30- or 365-day periods. That difference matters. Adding one month to 15 January gives 15 February, and adding twelve months gives the same day of the month a year later, but adding 365 days can land a day either side depending on whether a leap year falls in between. For deadlines and notice periods, count days. For anniversaries, renewals and billing cycles, use months or years.

One edge case is worth knowing. When the target month is shorter than the starting day, the day is clamped to the last valid date: 31 January plus one month becomes 28 February, or 29 February in a leap year. Years follow the same rule, so 29 February plus one year lands on 28 February.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I add 90 days to a date?

Enter the start date, choose Add, put 90 in the Days field and leave years and months at zero.

What happens when the target month is shorter?

The day is clamped to the last date in that month, so 31 January plus one month gives 28 February, or 29 February in a leap year. Nothing rolls over into the next month.

Are leap years handled?

Yes. The calculation uses real calendar dates, so 29 February exists in leap years and is skipped in others.

Can I combine years, months and days?

Yes. All three fields are applied together, in the order years, months, days.

Can I calculate a date in the past?

Choose Subtract instead of Add, and the same values are taken away from the start date.

What does the Days from today figure mean?

It is the difference between the result and today's date. A positive number is in the future, a negative number is in the past, and zero means today.
