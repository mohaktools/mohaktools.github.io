About the Energy Converter

The Energy Converter translates between joules, kilojoules, megajoules, calories, kilocalories, watt-hours, kilowatt-hours, BTU and electron-volts. Enter a value and pick the two units.

The joule is the SI unit, so everything else is defined against it. One calorie is 4.184 joules, and the calorie on a food label is actually a kilocalorie, a thousand of them, which is why a snack bar says 250 kcal rather than 250 calories. One kilowatt-hour, the unit on your electricity bill, is 3.6 megajoules. One BTU, used for air-conditioning, is about 1,055 joules.

The electron-volt belongs to particle physics, where it is a convenient size: roughly 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules. Because the units span so many orders of magnitude, results are shown in a compact form rather than with a fixed number of decimals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between calories and kilocalories?

A kilocalorie is a thousand calories. Food labels in most countries use kcal, sometimes written as Calories with a capital C.

How many joules are in a kilowatt-hour?

3.6 million, or 3.6 megajoules. That is why electricity is billed in kWh: one unit on the meter is one kWh.

What is a BTU?

The British thermal unit, about 1,055 joules. Air-conditioner capacity is often described in BTU per hour.

What is an electron-volt used for?

Atomic and particle physics, where energies are tiny. One eV is about 1.6 x 10^-19 joules.

Why is my electricity bill in units rather than joules?

One unit equals one kilowatt-hour, a practical size for household consumption. In joules the same figure would be huge.

Are these conversions exact?

The calorie and BTU factors are exact by definition. Results are rounded for display.
