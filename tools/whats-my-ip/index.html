About the What's My IP

What's My IP shows the public IP address your connection appears as, along with the city, region, country and network operator that address is registered to.

The address you see here is the one the internet sees, which is usually your router's or your mobile carrier's, not your device's. Local addresses in the 192.168.x.x or 10.x.x.x ranges are private and never leave your network.

The location shown is approximate. It comes from a database that maps address blocks to regions, so it can be the city where your ISP's equipment sits rather than where you are. A VPN or proxy changes the answer entirely, which is exactly why people use one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a public IP address?

The address your connection uses on the internet, assigned by your ISP. It is different from the private address inside your home network.

Why is the location wrong?

IP geolocation maps address blocks to regions. It is often the ISP's city, not yours, and can be wrong by a wide margin.

Does this show my device's local IP?

No. Only the public address the outside world sees.

Will a VPN change the result?

Yes. With a VPN the address shown belongs to the VPN provider, not your ISP.

Who provides the data?

A third-party IP lookup service, so the request is sent to that service.

Is my IP a secret?

No. Every site you visit sees it. It does, however, reveal your approximate location and ISP.
