About the Gratuity Calculator

The Gratuity Calculator estimates the lump sum an employer owes an employee at the end of continuous service. For establishments covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, the formula is fifteen days' wages for every completed year, which works out as last drawn basic plus dearness allowance, multiplied by 15/26, multiplied by the number of years of service.

The 26 in that fraction is the number of working days in a month, so fifteen days of wages is fifteen twenty-sixths of a month's salary. Part-years are rounded the way the Act requires: more than six months in the final year counts as a full year, and six months or less is dropped. Someone with 7 years and 8 months of service is therefore paid for 8 years.

Five years of continuous service is the usual eligibility threshold, and the calculator reports not eligible below that. The Act waives the five-year rule where service ends because of death or disablement. Gratuity up to twenty lakh rupees is exempt from income tax for employees covered by the Act, so the tool flags any result above that line.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the gratuity formula?

For covered establishments: last drawn basic plus DA, multiplied by 15/26, multiplied by the number of completed years of service.

Who is eligible for gratuity?

Employees of establishments with ten or more staff, after five years of continuous service. The five-year condition is waived on death or disablement.

How are part-years counted?

More than six months in the final year rounds up to a full year; six months or less is ignored. The calculator applies this rule automatically.

Is gratuity taxable?

Gratuity up to twenty lakh rupees is exempt for employees covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act. Anything above that is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.

Why is the multiplier 15/26?

Fifteen days of wages per year of service, with a month treated as 26 working days. Fifteen divided by twenty-six converts a monthly salary into fifteen days' worth.

Does it include bonus or allowances?

No. The statutory calculation uses basic pay and dearness allowance only. HRA, travel, special allowances and bonuses are excluded.
