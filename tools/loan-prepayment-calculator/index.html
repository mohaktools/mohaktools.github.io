About the Loan Prepayment Calculator

The Loan Prepayment Calculator shows what happens when you pay a lump sum against a loan while keeping the EMI unchanged. The EMI is recalculated from the outstanding principal and remaining tenure, then the reduced balance is spread over the same EMI to find how many months are left. The difference in total interest, and in months, is your saving.

Keeping the EMI fixed is the option that saves the most. Because interest is charged on the outstanding balance, a prepayment made early removes interest for every remaining year, while the same amount paid in the final years saves comparatively little. That is why a modest prepayment in year three of a twenty-year home loan can cut years off the term.

The calculation assumes a fixed rate and no charges. Floating-rate home loans to individuals carry no prepayment penalty in India, but fixed-rate loans and some other products may, so check before you pay. The tool also assumes the prepayment is made now rather than on a future date.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does prepayment reduce the tenure?

The lump sum cuts the outstanding principal. With the EMI held constant, the smaller balance is cleared sooner, so the remaining months fall.

What is the EMI formula?

EMI = P x r x (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1), where P is the principal, r the monthly interest rate and n the number of months.

Should I reduce the EMI or the tenure?

Reducing the tenure saves more interest, because the balance falls faster. Reducing the EMI lowers the monthly outflow instead. This calculator models the tenure option.

Are there prepayment charges?

Floating-rate home loans to individuals cannot carry a prepayment penalty in India. Fixed-rate loans and some personal or car loans can, so confirm with your lender first.

Why does an early prepayment save so much?

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance. Money paid early stops accruing interest for every year that remains, while the same amount paid near the end saves almost nothing.

What if I prepay more than the outstanding principal?

That is not a prepayment but a full closure. The calculator requires the prepayment to be smaller than the balance and asks you to check the figure.
