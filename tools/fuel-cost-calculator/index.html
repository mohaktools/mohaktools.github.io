About the Fuel Cost Calculator

The Fuel Cost Calculator turns a distance, a fuel efficiency figure and a fuel price into the two numbers that matter for a trip: how many litres you will burn and what it will cost. Fuel needed is the distance divided by efficiency, and cost is that figure multiplied by the price per litre.

The efficiency figure is where most estimates go wrong. Manufacturer claims and ARAI test numbers are measured in controlled conditions, so real-world consumption in city traffic is often 15-25% higher than the brochure figure. Using your own recent average, worked out from a full tank to a full tank, will give a far better answer than the sticker.

Remember that a return journey is double the one-way distance, and that tolls, parking and any idling time are outside this calculation. Petrol, diesel and CNG prices vary by city and change daily, so enter the price you actually paid rather than a national average.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is fuel cost calculated?

Litres needed = distance divided by efficiency in km per litre. Cost = litres multiplied by the price per litre.

Should I enter one-way or return distance?

Enter the full distance you will drive. For a return trip, add both legs together, so a 300 km one-way journey becomes 600 km.

Which mileage figure should I use?

Your own measured average is best. Company or ARAI figures are optimistic; real-world city driving is usually 15-25% thirstier.

Why is my actual cost higher than the result?

Traffic, air conditioning, a full load, under-inflated tyres and hilly roads all raise consumption. Tolls and parking are not included here either.

Can I use miles per gallon?

Not directly. Convert to km and km per litre first: 1 mile is about 1.609 km, and 1 mpg (US) is about 0.425 km per litre.

Does it work for CNG or electric vehicles?

For CNG, enter the price per kilogram and efficiency in km per kg. For electric vehicles the unit is kWh, so use a running-cost calculator instead.
