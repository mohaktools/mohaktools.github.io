About the Profit Margin Calculator

The Profit Margin Calculator takes a cost price and a selling price and returns the profit in rupees along with two percentages that are easy to confuse: margin and markup.

Margin is profit divided by the selling price, so it answers the question "what share of what I charge is profit?". Markup is profit divided by the cost, so it answers "how much have I added to what I paid?". Buy at 800 and sell at 1,200 and the profit is 400, which is a 33.33% margin but a 50% markup. The gap widens as the numbers grow, which is why quoting one as the other overstates or understates a business badly.

Margin can never reach 100%, because the selling price always includes the cost, but markup has no such ceiling. If you sell below cost both figures go negative, which the calculator reports rather than hiding. GST, shipping and marketplace fees are not included: enter prices on a consistent basis, ideally both excluding tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit as a share of the selling price. Markup is profit as a share of the cost. The same sale gives a lower margin percentage than markup.

How are the two calculated?

Margin = profit divided by selling price times 100. Markup = profit divided by cost price times 100.

What if I sell below cost?

Both figures turn negative, which correctly shows a loss. The calculator reports the negative numbers rather than treating them as an error.

Can profit margin be more than 100%?

No. The selling price always contains the cost, so margin stays below 100%. Markup, by contrast, can be any size.

How do I convert markup to margin?

Margin = markup divided by (100 + markup), times 100. A 50% markup is a 33.33% margin.

Does it include GST or shipping?

No. Enter cost and selling price on the same basis. For a true margin, use figures excluding GST and net of shipping and platform fees.
