About the Angle Converter

The Angle Converter changes an angle from one unit to another: degrees, radians, gradians, turns, arcminutes, arcseconds and milliradians. Enter the value, pick the two units, and the result appears immediately.

The units describe the same circle in different ways. One full turn is 360 degrees, 2π radians, 400 gradians or 6,400 milliradians. A degree splits into sixty arcminutes and each of those into sixty arcseconds, which is why coordinates in astronomy and surveying are written as degrees, minutes and seconds rather than decimals.

Radians are what mathematics actually uses, because they make derivatives and series behave cleanly, so any formula involving trigonometry expects them unless stated otherwise. Gradians survive mainly in surveying, where a right angle is a convenient 100. Milliradians are the artillery and optics unit: one mil subtends roughly a metre at a kilometre.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many radians are in a degree?

One degree is π/180 radians, about 0.017453. A full circle is 2π radians.

What is a gradian?

A unit where a full turn is 400, so a right angle is exactly 100. It is used in surveying and some European instruments.

What is a turn?

One complete rotation, equal to 360 degrees or 2π radians. It is used in engineering and in describing rotational speed.

Why use arcminutes and arcseconds?

They give precision without decimals. One degree is 60 arcminutes and one arcminute is 60 arcseconds, which suits coordinates and astronomy.

What is a milliradian used for?

Rangefinding and optics. One milliradian is about one metre at a distance of one kilometre.

Are the results rounded?

Yes, to a sensible number of decimal places for display. The underlying conversion is exact for the factor used.
