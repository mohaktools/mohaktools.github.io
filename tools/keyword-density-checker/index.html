About the Keyword Density Checker

The Keyword Density Checker counts how often each word appears in a piece of text and expresses that as a percentage of the whole. Paste an article, set a minimum word length to filter out noise, and it returns the most frequent words with their counts and densities.

Common English stop words, the the, and, of, is and their relatives, are excluded before counting, because they appear in every piece of writing and tell you nothing about the topic. The density figure is the word's count divided by the total number of words analysed, multiplied by a hundred.

Read the numbers as a sanity check rather than a target. There is no density percentage that Google rewards; writing that repeats a phrase often enough to hit a number reads badly and helps nobody. The useful signal here is accidental repetition, a word you did not realise you had used thirty times, and gaps, a topic you meant to cover that barely appears.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is keyword density?

The number of times a word appears, divided by the total word count, shown as a percentage.

What is a good keyword density?

There is no target figure. Natural writing usually lands between one and two per cent for its main topic; chasing a number produces repetitive text.

Why are common words missing from the results?

A built-in stop-word list removes words like the, and and of, because they appear everywhere and carry no topical meaning.

What does the minimum word length do?

It hides words shorter than the value you set, which filters out small filler words and keeps the list focused on meaningful terms.

Does it check phrases as well as single words?

No, this tool counts single words only. For phrase density you would need to search for the phrase and count occurrences manually.

Is my text sent to a server?

No. The analysis runs in the browser and the text stays on your device.
