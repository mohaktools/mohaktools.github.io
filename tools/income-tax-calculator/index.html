About the Income Tax Calculator (India)

The Income Tax Calculator estimates Indian income tax for the current financial year under both the new regime, which is the default, and the old regime. Enter your total annual income, pick a regime, and it returns taxable income, the tax at slab rates, surcharge and cess, and the total liability.

Under the new regime for FY 2026-27 the slabs run from nil up to four lakh, then 5% to eight lakh, 10% to twelve, 15% to sixteen, 20% to twenty, 25% to twenty-four, and 30% above that. A salaried taxpayer also gets the standard deduction of seventy-five thousand. Where total income is up to twelve lakh, the section 87A rebate of up to sixty thousand wipes out the tax entirely, and marginal relief stops the liability from jumping the moment income crosses that line.

The old regime keeps the two-and-a-half lakh exemption, a fifty-thousand standard deduction, and the deductions you enter, such as 80C and 80D. Surcharge is applied on high incomes and cess at four per cent on tax plus surcharge. This is an estimate for planning, not a return: it ignores marginal relief on surcharge, capital gains taxed at special rates, and anything specific to your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which financial year does this use?

FY 2026-27, the year covered by the Finance Act 2026 and the new Income-tax Act, 2025. The slabs were unchanged from FY 2025-26.

What are the new regime slabs?

Nil up to 4 lakh, 5% from 4 to 8 lakh, 10% from 8 to 12 lakh, 15% from 12 to 16 lakh, 20% from 16 to 20 lakh, 25% from 20 to 24 lakh and 30% above 24 lakh.

Is income up to 12 lakh really tax-free?

For a resident individual under the new regime, yes: the section 87A rebate of up to 60,000 cancels the slab tax at that level. Salaried taxpayers effectively reach 12.75 lakh because of the 75,000 standard deduction.

What is marginal relief?

Just above 12 lakh, the tax payable is capped at the amount by which income exceeds 12 lakh, so crossing the line by a rupee does not cost you 60,000. The calculator applies this for the new regime.

Which regime should I choose?

The new regime wins for most people because of the higher rebate and exemption, but the old regime can win if you claim large deductions for HRA, home loan interest, 80C and 80D. Run both and compare the totals.

How is surcharge applied?

10% above 50 lakh, 15% above 1 crore and 25% above 2 crore of taxable income. The old regime also has a 37% rate above 5 crore, which the new regime caps at 25%. Cess of 4% is charged on tax plus surcharge.
