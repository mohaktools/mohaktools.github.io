About the Color Background Remover

The Background Remover takes a photo and turns the background transparent. It works by colour: pick the shade to remove, set a tolerance, and every pixel close enough to that colour becomes transparent while the subject stays.

This is chroma-key removal, the same idea as a green screen. It works best on a plain, evenly lit background that differs clearly from the subject. It struggles with busy backgrounds, with hair or fur at the edges, and with subjects that share the background colour.

The tolerance slider is the important control. Too low and a halo of leftover background survives; too high and parts of the subject disappear with it. Nudge it until the edge looks clean, and remember the result is a PNG, since transparency needs an alpha channel that JPG does not have.

Common questions about Background Remover

How does the removal work?

Pixels within the tolerance of the chosen background colour are made transparent. Everything else is kept as it was.

What kind of images work best?

Plain, evenly lit backgrounds that contrast with the subject, such as a product on a white sheet or a person against a plain wall.

What does the tolerance setting do?

It sets how close a pixel must be to the background colour to be removed. Low values are strict, high values remove more but can eat into the subject.

Can it handle hair and fur?

Only roughly. Fine edges need a proper segmentation model, not colour matching.

What format should I save as?

PNG or WebP. JPG has no transparency, so a removed background becomes white.

Is my photo uploaded?

No. The processing happens on a canvas in your browser.
