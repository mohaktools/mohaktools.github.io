About the PDF Compressor

The PDF Compressor reduces a PDF's file size. Load a file, choose how hard to compress, and it rewrites the document with the content optimised, then reports the before and after sizes.

What can be reduced depends on what is inside. A PDF that is mostly scanned images shrinks a great deal, because the images can be recompressed. A PDF that is mostly text is already compact, so savings are small and further compression risks making it worse.

Compression is not lossless in the visual sense. Heavier settings reduce image quality, which is fine for an emailed invoice and not fine for a document that will be printed at size. Keep the original until you have checked the output.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much smaller will my PDF get?

Scanned, image-heavy PDFs often shrink by half or more. Text-based PDFs barely change.

Does compression reduce quality?

Heavier settings recompress images, which can soften them. Text stays sharp.

Why did my PDF get bigger?

Already-optimised files can be re-encoded inefficiently. Keep the smaller of the two.

Is my file uploaded?

No. The PDF is processed in your browser with pdf-lib, so nothing leaves your device.

Will it work on a password-protected PDF?

Usually not. Remove the password first.

Can it compress a scanned document with searchable text?

It can shrink the images, but heavy compression may damage the OCR text layer.
