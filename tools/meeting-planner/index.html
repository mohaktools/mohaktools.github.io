About the Meeting Planner

The Meeting Planner shows what a chosen date and time looks like in several time zones at once, so you can pick a slot that works for everyone.

The conversion uses your browser's built-in time-zone database, which knows about daylight saving. That matters: London shifts by an hour twice a year and New York by an hour in March and November, so a slot that worked last month may not work now.

Watch for zones with half-hour or forty-five-minute offsets. India is UTC+5:30, Nepal is UTC+5:45 and parts of Australia use UTC+9:30, so there is no whole-hour shortcut that works everywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does it handle daylight saving?

It uses the browser's time-zone data, which includes DST rules, so the offsets shown are correct for the chosen date.

Why is India on a half-hour offset?

IST is UTC+5:30, which keeps solar noon close to midday across the country without splitting it into two zones.

Does the date change in some zones?

Yes. A late evening slot in India is still the same day in Europe but can be the next morning in New Zealand.

How many zones can I compare?

Several at once. Add the locations your team is in.

Does it account for a country changing its DST rules?

Only as far as your browser's data is current. Very old browsers may be out of date.

Can I share the result?

Copy the times and send them. The page does not generate a link.
