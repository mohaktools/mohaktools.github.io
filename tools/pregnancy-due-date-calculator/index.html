About the Pregnancy Due Date Calculator

The Pregnancy Due Date Calculator applies Naegele's rule: take the first day of your last menstrual period, add 280 days, and adjust for the length of your cycle. It also returns an approximate conception date and the two trimester boundaries, so you can see how the forty weeks divide up.

The adjustment matters if your cycle is not the textbook 28 days. The rule assumes ovulation around day 14, so a 32-day cycle pushes the due date four days later, and a 26-day cycle pulls it two days earlier. The conception date shown here is LMP plus cycle length minus fourteen, which is the same assumption expressed differently.

Treat the result as a window rather than an appointment. Only about four per cent of babies arrive on the estimated date, and the great majority arrive within two weeks either side of it. A first-trimester ultrasound, which measures the baby directly, is more accurate than any calculation from dates, and an IVF pregnancy is dated from the transfer instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the due date calculated?

Naegele's rule: 280 days from the first day of your last menstrual period, adjusted by however much your cycle differs from 28 days.

What if my cycle is not 28 days?

Enter your average cycle length. Each day above 28 pushes the due date a day later, and each day below pulls it a day earlier.

How accurate is the estimated due date?

Roughly four per cent of births land on it, and most fall within two weeks either side. An early dating scan is more accurate than any date calculation.

What does the conception date mean here?

It is LMP plus cycle length minus fourteen, an estimate of ovulation. Actual conception can happen a few days either side of that.

When do the trimesters start?

This page marks the second trimester at 13 completed weeks and the third at 28 completed weeks, which is the usual clinical split.

Does it work for IVF or irregular cycles?

Not reliably. IVF pregnancies are dated from the embryo transfer, and irregular cycles are better dated by ultrasound. Ask your doctor for the official date.
