About the Sunrise & Sunset Calculator

The Sunrise and Sunset Calculator estimates when the sun rises and sets for an Indian city on a chosen date, along with daylight hours and solar noon.

The calculation uses the standard solar position equations: the day of the year sets the sun's declination, the city's latitude sets the hour angle, and the longitude corrected to the 82.5° meridian gives Indian Standard Time. Solar noon is the moment the sun is highest, which is not 12:00 on the clock because India spans a wide longitude range on a single time zone.

Expect accuracy within a few minutes. Published tables also correct for atmospheric refraction and the observer's altitude, which this approximation does not, so coastal and hill stations can differ slightly from an almanac.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the result?

Within a few minutes for most Indian cities. Published tables correct for refraction and elevation, which this does not.

Why is solar noon not at 12:00?

Because India uses one time zone across a wide longitude range. Solar noon happens earlier in the east and later in the west.

Does daylight saving affect this?

No. India does not observe daylight saving, so IST is constant all year.

Why does daylight length change through the year?

The earth's axial tilt changes how much of each day the sun spends above the horizon, more so at higher latitudes.

Can I use it outside India?

The city list is Indian and the time correction assumes IST. For other countries the times would need a different meridian.

What is solar noon?

The moment the sun reaches its highest point for that day, halfway between sunrise and sunset.
