About the Overtime Calculator

The Overtime Calculator splits a day's hours into regular time and overtime, then prices both. Hours up to your regular daily limit are paid at the normal hourly rate; everything beyond it is paid at the rate multiplied by the overtime factor you choose.

The multiplier is the part that varies. Time and a half is the common commercial figure, but India's Factories Act requires twice the ordinary rate of wages for workers who put in more than nine hours in a day or forty-eight hours in a week, so factory staff should select 2x. A quarter-time uplift is occasionally used for shift or holiday arrangements.

This is a per-day calculation, so it does not track a weekly total. If your overtime is judged weekly rather than daily, add the week's hours first and compare against your weekly limit instead of using this page. Overtime pay is fully taxable as part of salary, and it also counts towards provident fund and gratuity calculations at most employers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is overtime pay calculated?

Hours up to the regular daily limit are paid at the hourly rate. Hours beyond it are paid at the hourly rate multiplied by the overtime factor, and the two are added together.

Which multiplier should I use?

1.5x is the usual commercial figure. Factory workers covered by the Factories Act are entitled to twice the ordinary rate, so choose 2x if that applies to you.

Is overtime pay taxable?

Yes. It is part of salary income and taxed at your slab rate. It also usually counts towards provident fund and gratuity.

Does it handle weekly overtime?

No, this page works on a single day. For weekly overtime, add the week's hours and compare them with your weekly limit, then price the excess separately.

What should I enter as regular hours?

Your normal shift length, usually 8 hours. The Factories Act sets 9 hours a day and 48 hours a week as the thresholds for overtime.

Are there legal limits on overtime?

Yes. Labour law caps how much overtime can be worked, so a large figure here does not mean it is lawful to work it. Check your state's rules and your contract.
