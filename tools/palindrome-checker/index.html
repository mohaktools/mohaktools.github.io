About the Palindrome Checker

The Palindrome Checker tells you whether a word, phrase or number reads the same forwards and backwards. It also shows the reversed version, so you can see exactly why a phrase passes or fails.

With the ignore-case option on, the comparison lowercases the text first, which is what makes phrases like Never odd or even count. Spaces and punctuation are stripped for the comparison, since a palindrome is about the letters and digits, not the formatting around them.

Numbers work the same way, so 12321 is a palindrome and 12345 is not. Palindromic phrases are a staple of puzzles and wordplay; the longest single-word examples in English run to several letters, and constructed sentences can be far longer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a palindrome?

A word, phrase or number that reads identically forwards and backwards, such as level, madam or 12321.

Does it ignore spaces and punctuation?

Yes, they are removed before comparing, so a phrase like A man, a plan, a canal, Panama passes.

Is the check case sensitive?

You choose. With the ignore-case option on, case differences do not affect the result.

Do numbers work?

Yes, digits are compared the same way as letters, so 12321 is reported as a palindrome.

Why does my phrase fail when it looks symmetrical?

Usually a hidden character, a hyphen or an accented letter that does not reverse cleanly. Check the reversed output to see what changed.

What is the longest palindrome?

Single-word examples in English run to several letters, and deliberately constructed sentences can be much longer.
