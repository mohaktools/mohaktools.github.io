About the Water Intake Calculator

The Water Intake Calculator estimates a daily drinking target from body weight, activity level and climate. The base is thirty-five millilitres per kilogram of body weight, which is the most widely used rule of thumb, and the activity and climate selections add a fixed allowance on top.

For a 70 kg adult with moderate exercise in a normal climate that works out to about 2.9 litres, which sits comfortably inside the two to three litres most guidance suggests. The glass count converts the total into 250 ml servings, because a target in litres is hard to act on and a count of glasses is not.

Two caveats are worth keeping in mind. Food supplies roughly a fifth of the water you take in, so the drinking target does not need to cover total needs on its own, and tea, coffee and milk count towards it as well. Requirements rise in heat, at altitude, during illness and with breastfeeding, and fall in some kidney or heart conditions where fluid is restricted. If you have a medical condition affecting fluid balance, follow your doctor's advice over any calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the daily target calculated?

Thirty-five millilitres per kilogram of body weight, plus a fixed allowance for your activity level and another for a hot or humid climate.

Is eight glasses a day correct for everyone?

No. It is a rough average. Needs scale with body size, activity and climate, which is why a 50 kg sedentary adult and a 90 kg labourer should not have the same target.

Do tea, coffee and other drinks count?

Yes, mostly. They contribute to daily fluid intake. Very strong caffeine intake has a mild diuretic effect, but it does not cancel the fluid in the drink.

Does food count towards this?

Yes. Food typically supplies about a fifth of total water intake, so the drinking target does not need to cover everything on its own.

Can I drink too much water?

In rare cases, yes. Drinking several litres in a short time can dilute blood sodium dangerously. Spread your intake across the day rather than forcing large volumes.

Does this apply to children or pregnant women?

Not directly. Children, pregnant and breastfeeding women have different needs, and the weight-based rule here is not a substitute for medical guidance.
