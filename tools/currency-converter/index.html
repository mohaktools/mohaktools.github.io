About the Currency Converter

The Currency Converter changes an amount from one currency to another using exchange rates fetched live from a public rates API. Enter the amount, pick the source and target currency, and it shows both the converted figure and the rate it used.

The rate shown is the mid-market rate, the midpoint between buy and sell prices on the wholesale market. That is the honest reference number, but it is not what you will get. Banks, cards and exchange counters add a spread, typically one to three per cent, so the amount that actually lands in your account is lower.

If the rates cannot be fetched, the tool falls back to a small built-in table of approximate rates so it still gives a rough answer, and says so on the page. Those offline figures are fixed at the time the page was written and drift as markets move, so treat them as indicative only.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do the exchange rates come from?

A public exchange-rate API is queried when you convert. The page shows the date the rates were last updated.

How often are the rates updated?

Once a day on the free tier. Intraday movement is not reflected.

Why does my bank give a worse rate?

Banks and card networks add a margin of roughly one to three per cent over the mid-market rate, and some charge a separate fee.

What happens if I am offline?

The tool falls back to a small set of approximate built-in rates and tells you the figure is an estimate.

Does it work for every currency?

The live API covers most world currencies. The offline fallback covers only ten.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a reference conversion. For anything involving a transfer or a trade, use the rate your provider quotes.
