About the Regex Tester

The Regex Tester runs a regular expression against your sample text and highlights what it matches, live as you type. Add flags for case-insensitive, global or multiline matching, and optionally a replacement string to see the substituted result.

Regular expressions describe a pattern rather than a literal string, which is what makes them powerful and unreadable in equal measure. \d is a digit, \w a word character, \s whitespace, and the quantifiers * + ? and {n,m} say how many times the previous thing may repeat.

Two practical warnings. Special characters such as . * + ? ( ) [ ] need escaping with a backslash when you mean them literally. And some patterns, particularly nested quantifiers like (a+)+, can backtrack catastrophically and hang on certain inputs, so avoid them on untrusted text.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do the flags do?

g finds all matches rather than the first, i ignores case, and m makes ^ and $ match the start and end of each line.

Why does my pattern match nothing?

Usually an unescaped special character. A literal dot needs \. and a literal bracket needs \[.

Can I test a replacement?

Yes. Enter the replacement string and the tool shows the substituted output, with $1 referring to the first capture group.

Does it support lookbehind?

Modern browsers support (?<=...) and (?

What is catastrophic backtracking?

A pattern that explores exponentially many ways to fail, freezing the browser. Nested quantifiers are the usual cause.

Is my text uploaded?

No. Matching happens in the browser.
