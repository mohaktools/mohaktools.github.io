About the GPA Calculator

The GPA Calculator converts a list of course grades and credit hours into a credit-weighted grade point average. Each grade is turned into a grade point on the ten-point scale, multiplied by the credits that course carries, and the total is divided by the credits attempted. A four-credit course therefore moves your GPA twice as much as a two-credit one.

The grade points follow the common Indian ten-point convention: O and A+ are worth 10, A is 9, B+ is 8, B is 7, C+ is 6, C is 5, D is 4 and F is 0. Letter grades with a minus or plus from the US system are accepted too, with A- at 9, B- at 7, C- at 5 and D+ at 4. Add a row for each course, enter its credits, and the tool ignores nothing silently: an unrecognised grade stops the calculation and tells you which one it could not read.

The four-point figure is a straight linear conversion of the ten-point result, so a 9.0 becomes 3.6. Real US universities use their own conversion tables, and WES and other credential evaluators use theirs, so treat the four-point number as a rough guide rather than an official equivalent.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is GPA calculated?

Each grade is converted to a grade point, multiplied by the course credits, and the products are added. That total is divided by the total credits attempted.

Which grade scale does it use?

The ten-point scale: O and A+ = 10, A and A- = 9, B+ = 8, B and B- = 7, C+ = 6, C and C- = 5, D and D+ = 4, F = 0.

How is the four-point GPA worked out?

By scaling the ten-point GPA by four-tenths, so 8.5 becomes 3.4. It is an approximation; universities and credential evaluators use their own tables.

How do I handle a pass/fail course?

Leave it out. A pass/fail course usually carries credits but no grade point, so including it would distort the average.

What if a course carries zero credits?

It contributes nothing to the result either way. The credit total is what the division uses, so zero-credit rows are harmless.

Can I calculate CGPA with this?

Yes, if you enter one row per semester with the semester's SGPA as the grade and the semester's credits as the credits. The result is the credit-weighted CGPA.
