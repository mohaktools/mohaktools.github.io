How to Create a Strong Password (and Check If It Is Strong)

Everyday Utilities · Published 15 November 2026

Most account breaches start with weak or reused passwords. This guide explains what makes a password hard to guess, how to build one you can remember, and the simple habits that protect your accounts.

A password is the lock on your email, bank, social media and shopping accounts. If one password is weak, or if you use the same one everywhere, a leak on one site can open many doors. The good news is that a few simple habits remove most of the risk.

What makes a password strong

  • Length: longer is much better. Aim for at least 12 characters, and more for important accounts.
  • Unpredictability: random mixes are harder to guess than a real word with a number at the end.
  • Uniqueness: every account should have its own password, so one leak does not affect the rest.

Why common tricks fail

Passwords like Summer2026! or P@ssw0rd look complicated but follow patterns that attackers test first. Swapping letters for symbols or adding a number at the end does not help much. Attackers use lists of common words and these exact substitutions.

Two ways to build a strong password

Option 1: A random password

Use a password generator to create a string of random letters, numbers and symbols. This is the strongest option, and you do not need to remember it if you save it in a password manager.

Option 2: A passphrase

A passphrase is several unrelated words joined together, such as four or five random words with a separator. It is long and easier to remember than random characters. Pick the words at random rather than from a line of a song or a quote you know well.

Example: weak and strong

Weak: Rohan1995 (a name and a year, easy to guess from social media)

Stronger: a random 16-character password from a generator, or a passphrase of four random words

Simple steps you can follow today

  1. Change the passwords on your email account first, because email can reset most other accounts.
  2. Generate a unique password for each important account and save it in a password manager.
  3. Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it is offered, especially for email, banking and social media.
  4. Check a password you are unsure about with a strength checker before you use it.

Common mistakes to avoid

Reusing the same password

This is the biggest risk. If one site is breached, criminals try the same email and password on other sites.

Writing passwords on paper next to your computer

Store them in a password manager or in a locked place away from your device.

Changing passwords without a reason

Change a password when there is a sign of a breach or a suspicious login, not on a fixed schedule that leads to small, predictable edits.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a strong password be?

At least 12 characters is a good minimum. Longer passwords, or passphrases of four or more random words, are harder to guess.

Is a password generator safe?

A generator is only as safe as the way it works. Check that the tool runs in your browser and does not send the password to a server before you use it for an important account.

Should I use a password manager?

Yes. A password manager lets you use a different strong password for every account without memorising them all.

Do I need two-factor authentication if I have a strong password?

Yes. Two-factor authentication adds a second check, so a stolen password alone is not enough to get into your account.

Conclusion

A strong password is long, random and unique to each account. Use a generator or a passphrase, store the passwords in a manager, and turn on two-factor authentication for your most important accounts.
