BMI Explained: What Your Score Means and How to Calculate It

Calculators · Published 15 November 2026

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a quick way to check whether your weight is in a healthy range for your height. This guide explains how it is calculated, what the categories mean, and why BMI is only one part of the picture.

BMI is one of the most common health checks because it needs only your height and weight. It is not a diagnosis, but it gives a useful starting point for a conversation with a doctor or a plan to build healthier habits.

The BMI formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

Convert your height to metres first. For example, 175 cm becomes 1.75 m. Then square it and divide your weight by that number.

Example

A person weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall.

  • Height squared = 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625
  • BMI = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9

If you prefer pounds and inches, multiply your weight in pounds by 703 and divide by your height in inches squared. The result is the same.

The standard categories

The World Health Organization uses these adult ranges:

  • Below 18.5: underweight
  • 18.5 to 24.9: healthy weight range
  • 25 to 29.9: overweight
  • 30 and above: obese

Some health bodies in Asia use lower cut-offs, because health risks can rise at lower BMI values for many South Asian populations. If you are in India, your doctor may use those guidelines when they look at your result.

What BMI does not tell you

  • Muscle and fat are counted together. A muscular person can have a high BMI and still be healthy.
  • Fat location matters. Fat around the waist carries more risk than fat elsewhere. Measure your waist as well.
  • Age and sex change the picture. Older adults and children are assessed differently.

Common mistakes to avoid

Using height in centimetres directly

The formula needs metres. Using 175 instead of 1.75 gives a number that is far too small to mean anything.

Treating one reading as a verdict

Your weight changes through the day. Measure at about the same time, and look at trends over several weeks rather than one number.

Ignoring food and water when planning changes

If you are working on your weight, it helps to know your daily energy needs and how much water you drink. Those two numbers are as useful as the BMI itself.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults, the standard healthy range is 18.5 to 24.9. Some Asian health guidelines use lower thresholds, so ask a doctor for advice that fits you.

Can I calculate BMI in pounds and inches?

Yes. Multiply your weight in pounds by 703, then divide by your height in inches squared.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle adds weight, so athletes can have a high BMI and still have low body fat. Other measures give a better picture for them.

Does BMI diagnose any condition?

No. BMI is a screening tool. It suggests whether a closer look is needed, but a doctor must assess your health.

Conclusion

BMI is a simple, quick check that uses only height and weight. Use it as a starting point, read it alongside your waist measurement and overall health, and talk to a doctor if the result worries you.
