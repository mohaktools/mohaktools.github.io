SIP vs Fixed Deposit: Which Gives Better Returns?

Calculators · Published 15 November 2026

Both SIPs and fixed deposits are popular ways to save, but they work very differently. One gives a fixed return, the other depends on the market. Here is how to compare them with real numbers.

Many people ask which is better, a monthly SIP or a fixed deposit (FD). The honest answer is that they are built for different jobs. An FD is about certainty. A SIP is about long-term growth with ups and downs along the way.

What a fixed deposit gives you

An FD lets you lock a lump sum with a bank for a fixed period at a fixed interest rate. The rate is agreed upfront, so you know roughly what you will receive at maturity. The interest is taxable as per your slab, and early withdrawal often reduces the return.

What a SIP gives you

A SIP, or Systematic Investment Plan, is a monthly investment into a mutual fund. Your money buys units at the market price each month. Over time this can grow a lot, but the returns are not guaranteed, and the value can fall in some years.

The formulas

For a lump sum in an FD that compounds, the maturity amount is:

Maturity = P × (1 + r/n)n × t

Where P is the amount deposited, r is the annual rate, n is how many times it compounds per year, and t is the number of years.

For a monthly SIP, the approximate future value is:

Future value = M × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)

Where M is the monthly investment, i is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of months.

Example 1: A lump sum in an FD

You deposit ₹1,00,000 at 7% a year, compounded quarterly, for 5 years. Using the formula with r = 0.07, n = 4 and t = 5, the maturity is about ₹1,41,500. That is roughly ₹41,500 in interest, and you knew this figure from the start.

Example 2: A monthly SIP

You invest ₹5,000 every month for 10 years, and assume a steady 12% annual return. The total you put in is ₹6,00,000. Using the formula with M = 5,000, i = 0.01 and n = 120, the value is about ₹11.6 lakh.

This number depends entirely on the 12% assumption. Real markets do not give the same return every year. If the average return were 8%, the same SIP would end up noticeably lower. Always check a few return scenarios, not just the best one.

How to decide

  • Emergency money or money needed within 1-3 years: an FD is usually the safer choice, because the value is predictable.
  • Long-term goals like retirement or a child's education: a SIP has more room to grow, but you need patience through market falls.
  • Both: many people keep an FD for short-term safety and a SIP for long-term growth.

Common mistakes to avoid

Comparing a guaranteed rate with a market return

An FD rate is fixed, while a SIP return is an estimate. Compare them using a range of possible SIP returns, not a single number.

Ignoring tax and charges

Interest on FDs is taxable, and mutual funds have their own tax rules and expense ratios. Check the current rules before you decide, because these can change.

Stopping a SIP during a market fall

Stopping when the market drops locks in the loss. A SIP works best when you keep investing through the ups and downs.

Frequently asked questions

Is a SIP better than a fixed deposit?

It depends on your goal and time frame. A SIP can grow more over long periods but its return is not guaranteed. An FD gives a fixed return that is easier to plan around.

Is an FD return guaranteed?

The rate is fixed for the agreed period, and the bank pays it as long as the deposit is held to maturity. Early withdrawal terms can reduce the return.

How is SIP return calculated?

The return depends on the market. Calculators estimate the future value using an assumed annual return, which you can change to test different outcomes.

Should I stop my SIP when the market falls?

Stopping during a fall often locks in losses. Many investors keep the SIP running, because it buys more units when prices are lower.

Conclusion

FDs suit money you need to be safe and predictable. SIPs suit long-term goals where you can accept short-term ups and downs. Test both with your own figures, and keep the time frame of each goal in mind when you choose.
