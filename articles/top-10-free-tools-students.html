Top 10 Free Online Tools for Students in 2026

Students · Published 29 November 2026

You do not need to pay for software to study better. These ten free online tools cover writing, maths, research, citations, and focus, so you can spend more time learning and less time searching.

Students often spend hours on small tasks: counting words, checking a sum, formatting a reference, or staying focused for one more hour. The right tools make these tasks quick. Below are ten free options, from MohakTools and other well-known services, with a short note on what each one is best for.

1. Word counter

Assignments usually have word limits. Paste your draft into a word counter to see words, characters with and without spaces, and lines. It takes a few seconds and helps you stay within the limit.

2. Basic calculator

For quick arithmetic during lab work or homework checks, a basic calculator is enough. It supports decimals, percentages, and keyboard input, so you can type your numbers directly.

3. Percentage calculator

Percentages come up in marks, statistics, and science reports. The percentage calculator helps you find what percent a score is, or how much a value has changed. For example, if you scored 42 out of 50, it shows 84 percent.

4. Age calculator

Useful for school forms, applications, and quick date checks. The age calculator finds the exact time between two dates, in years, months, and days.

5. Time converter

If you are working on physics or engineering problems, you often need to convert units of time. The time converter handles common conversions in a few steps.

6. Pomodoro timer

The Pomodoro technique splits study time into focused blocks, usually 25 minutes of work followed by a short break. A timer keeps you on track without checking your phone. You can find a timer in the MohakTools clocks and timers section.

7. Google Scholar

For research papers, Google Scholar is the best free starting point. It searches academic articles, theses, and books across many fields. You can save papers, see who cites them, and export citations.

8. Zotero

Zotero is a free reference manager. It saves sources from your browser and creates citations and bibliographies in APA, MLA, and other formats. If you write long papers, it saves hours of manual formatting.

9. Desmos

Desmos is a free online graphing calculator. Type an equation and see the graph instantly. It is very helpful for algebra, calculus, and understanding how functions behave when you change a value.

10. Password generator

Students sign up for many accounts, from school portals to online courses. A password generator creates strong, random passwords so you do not reuse the same one everywhere. Pair it with a password manager to store them safely.

Bonus: Many of these tools are free for basic use but offer paid plans with extra features. The free versions are enough for most student tasks.

How to choose the right tools

You do not need all ten at once. Start with the tools that match your biggest problem. If you struggle with word limits, begin with the word counter. If research takes most of your time, try Google Scholar and Zotero. If you lose focus, use a timer.
